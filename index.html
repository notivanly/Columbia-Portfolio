Bridge Physics Simulator

Send a car, a pickup or a heavy truck across a truss bridge and see which bars take the load.

Try Drag the vehicle to the middle of the span, then tap a bar.

Vehicle

Open the bridge game: Bridge Physics Simulator (opens in a new tab)
How it works

On this page. A Warren truss bridge, 16 m long. The load of each axle is shared between the two deck joints nearest to it, and the bar forces are found with the stiffness method for a pin-jointed truss every time the vehicle moves. Tension is positive. The vehicle masses are 1.5 t (car), 2.5 t (pickup) and 18 t (heavy truck), and the load slider scales them.

What is exaggerated. The real movement of the bridge is a few millimetres, so the drawn deflection is magnified (x400 for the car, x250 for the pickup, x40 for the truck) and the picture says so. The springs under the vehicle body are decoration: they do not enter the calculation.

Linked project. A separate bridge-building game, titled Load Limit on its page: five materials on a budget, vehicles from a bicycle to a train, templates, lessons, a force diagram, and wind, earthquake and rain tests. The exploded view on the home screen is decorative.

Mini Wind Tunnel Simulator

Pick an object, change the wind speed and its angle, and watch the air and the forces respond.

Try Choose the banana, then tilt it.

Object

Open the simulator: Mini Wind Tunnel Simulator (opens in a new tab)Source code: Mini Wind Tunnel Simulator on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

What you see. A side view of a small test section. The streamlines come from potential flow (an ideal fluid with no viscosity), solved with a source-panel method for each object’s own outline, plus an illustrative wake drawn behind it. The airfoil uses a Joukowski section with the Kutta condition; the sphere, cylinder, car and banana are simpler flows around their silhouettes, so none of them reuses the airfoil model.

Numbers. Drag and lift come from approximate coefficients: about 0.47 for a smooth sphere, 0.8 to 0.95 for a cylinder depending on how it is turned, about 0.30 for a car at zero pitch, and a thin-airfoil lift slope with a simple stall rule for the wing. There is no published drag coefficient for a banana, so its value is an estimate and is labelled that way. Forces are for a small model, 0.05 m per world unit.

Limits. This is not CFD: no viscosity, no boundary layer, no real flow separation or turbulence. The airflow is illustrative and the coefficients are approximate.

Linked project. A separate simulator with controls for wind speed, angle of attack, air density and wing area, and readouts for lift, drag and their ratio.

Truss Force Analyzer

Drag a joint, move the load, remove a bar, and see every force worked out again.

Try Drag a joint of the unbraced frame, or take a bar out of the Pratt truss.

Truss

Move a joint without dragging

Open the analyzer: Truss Force Analyzer (opens in a new tab)Source code: Truss Force Analyzer on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Model. K u = F. The solver builds the stiffness matrix of a pin-jointed 2D truss, solves it by Gaussian elimination for how far each joint moves, and turns each bar’s change in length into an axial force. Tension is positive, compression negative, and the support reactions come from the same solution. It handles determinate and indeterminate trusses and is run again after every drag, load change or removed bar.

When there is no answer. If a joint is loose, there is no support, two joints coincide, or the arrangement can move without stretching a bar (a mechanism, like the unbraced frame), the page shows a short message and no forces at all, because there is no valid result to show. A truss so nearly unstable that its joints would move too far for this small-movement model is reported the same way.

Limits. Bars are linear elastic and share one axial stiffness, displacements are small, and the drawn movement is magnified and labelled. Member strength and buckling are not checked.

Linked project. The separate Truss Force Analyzer, where you draw your own truss, add supports and loads, and export results as CSV.

Physics Sandbox

A small room with gravity, bounce and friction. Grab something and throw it.

Try Pause, line up the dominoes, then press Play.

Gravity
Start from

Open the sandbox: Physics Sandbox (opens in a new tab)Source code: Physics Sandbox on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Engine. Rigid circles and boxes in a 9 m by 6 m room. Contacts are found with the separating axis test and solved with sequential impulses, including friction and warm starting, in 120 Hz substeps. When you grab a body, a soft constraint pulls the point you hold toward the pointer, so letting go throws it with the speed it had. The number of objects is capped at 48.

Presets. Free play, a pendulum on a rigid rod, a row of dominoes, a stack and a pyramid. Each one only uses what the engine does well. While paused, dragging simply moves a body, so a set-up can be arranged first.

Limits. 2D only, no continuous collision detection, so very fast objects can pass through thin ones.

Linked project. The separate Physics Sandbox, with joints (hinge, rod, rope and spring), polygons and more scenes.

Planet Explorer

Pick a system, then click a planet to see it up close beside an Earth.

Try Open TRAPPIST-1, then speed up time.

System

Open Planetary Gravity Explorer: Planetary Gravity Explorer (opens in a new tab)Source code: Planetary Gravity Explorer on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Data. Solar System: the NASA Planetary Fact Sheet (diameters, masses, distances from the Sun, orbital periods, surface gravity). TRAPPIST-1: masses, periods and distances from Agol et al. 2021 (Planetary Science Journal) and radii from the NASA Exoplanet Archive. Lantern is a made-up system, labelled as fictional everywhere it appears, and its numbers are invented.

What is not to scale. The overview is a conceptual layout: orbit distances are compressed on a logarithmic scale so every orbit can be seen, and the planets are enlarged. The close-up shows the planet next to an Earth at the same scale. Planets move at their real relative orbital periods; the time speed is a multiple of a typical pace for that system.

Appearance. Colours and features of the Solar System’s planets follow what spacecraft and telescope images show, simplified. No surface has been observed on any TRAPPIST-1 planet, so those are plain tinted spheres: the colours only tell them apart.

Linked project. The separate Planetary Gravity Explorer compares surface gravity and escape velocity across the eight planets.

Orbital Mechanics Simulator

Launch a craft, burn prograde or retrograde, and watch the predicted path change.

Try Burn prograde once, then read the new high point.

Orbit around

Technical details

Units: kilometres, kilometres per second, seconds. Fourth-order Runge-Kutta, with the step shortened near the body. Changing the starting height or speed restarts the flight from the launch point.

Open the simulator: Orbital Mechanics Simulator (opens in a new tab)Source code: Orbital Mechanics Simulator on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Model. Two-body gravity, a = −GM r / |r|³, advanced from the craft’s actual position and velocity with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integrator in kilometres and seconds. A burn changes the speed along the direction of travel. Reaching the surface ends the flight as an impact; a path with positive orbital energy that leaves 30 body radii ends it as an escape. Reset returns to the launch point.

Prediction. The dashed path is found by stepping a copy of the current state forward for one orbit (or until it hits the body), so it follows every burn.

Constants. GM and radius for Earth (398,600.4 km³/s², 6,378.1 km), the Moon (4,902.8, 1,737.4) and Mars (42,828.4, 3,396.2).

Limits. One body, no atmosphere, a point-mass craft. It is not for mission planning.

Linked project. The separate Orbital Mechanics Simulator.

AMC Practice

Timed AMC 8, 10 and 12 mock tests, problems by topic, drills with worked solutions, and progress tracking.

Try Start a mock test and review every question afterward.

Explore tool: AMC Practice (opens in a new tab)Source code: AMC Practice on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Preview. A capture of the site, taken from a local copy of its repository, so fonts can differ slightly from the live page.

Title. It is titled AMC Arena on the page.

Content. 133 AMC-style problems across algebra, geometry, number theory, and counting and probability, plus generated drills.

Scoring. The AMC 8 scores 1 point per correct answer. The AMC 10 and 12 score 6 for a correct answer, 1.5 for a blank and 0 for a wrong answer.

Implementation. A static site with no server. Progress is saved in your browser’s local storage, and math is drawn with KaTeX. The problem bank is written in Python and built into problems.js by a script that checks each problem has five choices and a valid answer.

Note. It is an unofficial practice site, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Mathematical Association of America.

Calculus III Visualizer

Graph a function of x and y as a 3D surface and explore its tangent plane, gradient and level curves.

Try Type your own function, then click the surface.

Explore tool: Calculus III Visualizer (opens in a new tab)Source code: Calculus III Visualizer on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Preview. A capture of the site, taken from a local copy of its repository, so fonts can differ slightly from the live page.

Implementation. Built with Three.js, loaded from a CDN. The functions you type are read by a small expression parser, and the surface is sampled on a 120 by 120 grid. The page notes that derivatives are computed numerically.

Gradient. The gradient is the pair of partial derivatives, (fx, fy), drawn as an arrow on the function you typed. Moving the point shows how the arrow and the tangent plane change.

Vector fields. You enter the components P, Q and R of a field, which is drawn as arrows with animated flow and a probe point. The page also covers divergence and curl.

Limits. It needs an internet connection to load Three.js.

AP Atlas

Independent concept review for the 2027 AP course list, with untimed practice, timed review sets, answer review and local progress.

Try Open a subject and try a timed review set.

Explore tool: AP Atlas (opens in a new tab)Source code: AP Atlas on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
How it works

Preview. A capture of the site, taken from a local copy of its repository, so fonts can differ slightly from the live page.

Content. 42 subjects, with 2,352 question variants generated from 588 original concept and definition pairs, and 84 timed review sets of 28 questions in 35 minutes. Sets share underlying concepts: they are not full-length official-format exams, and the bank is a starting point, not a predictor of exam scores.

Implementation. A static site with no dependencies. Progress is stored in your browser. The question bank is written in a text file and built into data.js by a Python script.

Note. It is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by the College Board.
